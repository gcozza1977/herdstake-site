Not paid for cattle?

Mind the trust deadlines

Packers and dealers must pay for livestock before the close of the next business day after the purchase and transfer of possession, unless the seller agreed in writing beforehand to other terms (7 U.S.C. § 228b). Cattle a packer or dealer buys in a cash sale, and the proceeds, are held in trust for unpaid cash sellers (7 U.S.C. § 196 for packers, § 217b for dealers).

The seller keeps the trust only by giving written notice to the buyer and filing it with USDA within 30 days after the final date for payment, or within 15 business days after learning that a payment check or instrument was dishonored. A cash sale is one where the seller did not expressly extend credit; credit sales are not covered. Packers averaging $500,000 or less in annual purchases, and dealers averaging $100,000 or less, are exempt.

Weekends are skipped when counting business days; federal holidays are not, so a deadline near a holiday may come a day or more later. Count conservatively and file early. General information, not legal advice: talk to a lawyer about your own sale. How to file: USDA AMS, how to file a bond claim (PDF) and USDA's Packers and Stockyards Division.

About the data

Each result shows the name and DBA exactly as USDA lists them, city and state, the type of registrant, the active bond coverage and, for dealers, any clearor bond, with the list's own "current as of" date and a link to the file. We show no street addresses, phone numbers or emails. Lists are refreshed monthly.

A name missing from these lists doesn't mean anything is wrong: registration can be pending, or the business may be listed under another name. Ask the buyer for its USDA registration number and confirm with the Packers and Stockyards Division. USDA's national expired-registration file currently holds a single 2019 entry, so it is not included here. Corrections: team@herdstake.com.
