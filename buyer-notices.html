Buyer-notice kit · free

Keep your lien on cattle that sell.

When a borrower sells cattle, your security interest follows the sale only if the buyer was put on notice the way federal law requires. Here's how that works in Texas, Kansas and Nebraska, with a notice template and a log to keep notices current.

Which system applies

Central filing or direct notice

StateSystemWhat the lender does
NebraskaCentral filing, certified by USDAFile an effective financing statement (EFS) with the Secretary of State. Buyers who register with the state receive the lists. An EFS doesn't replace your UCC financing statement.
TexasDirect noticeSend written notice to each buyer, commission merchant and selling agent the borrower may sell to.
KansasDirect noticeSame as Texas.

The rules come from the Food Security Act of 1985 (7 U.S.C. § 1631). Only 19 states run a USDA-certified central filing system. Cattle that cross state lines can raise harder questions; ask counsel.

Direct notice

What the notice must contain

  1. Your name and address, as the secured party.
  2. The borrower's name and address.
  3. The borrower's Social Security number or other approved unique identifier, or for a business, its IRS taxpayer identification number.
  4. A description of the cattle subject to your security interest: the amount where it applies, the county, and a reasonable description of the property.
  5. Any payment terms you set for releasing your lien, such as a check made out jointly to the borrower and you.

Timing. The buyer must have received the notice within one year before the sale, so send it again before it turns a year old. Amend it in writing within three months of a material change. It lapses when it expires or when you send a signed notice that it has lapsed, whichever comes first. Notices are organized by farm product.

The buyer list

Make the borrower tell you who's buying

Your security agreement can require the borrower to list the buyers, commission merchants and selling agents they may sell to. If the borrower then sells to someone not on the list, federal law requires them to tell you in writing at least 7 days before the sale, or account to you for the proceeds within 10 days. Otherwise they face a fine of $5,000 or 15% of the value received, whichever is greater.

A routine that holds up
  • Get the buyer list at closing and at every renewal: sale barns, order buyers, feedyards and packers.
  • Send notices so you can prove receipt, such as certified mail with a return receipt, and log the date received.
  • Re-send before each notice is a year old, and whenever the borrower adds a buyer.
  • Check that cattle proceeds arrive with your name on the check.

These are Herdstake's suggestions, not legal requirements.

Template

A notice you can adapt

NOTICE OF SECURITY INTEREST IN FARM PRODUCTS Given under 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e) Date: [date] To: [buyer, commission merchant or selling agent] [address] Farm product: Cattle Secured party (lender): [name] [address] Debtor (borrower): [name] [address] Debtor's Social Security number or other approved unique identifier (for a business, its IRS taxpayer identification number): [number] Description of the farm products: [number of head and class, e.g. 1,200 head of feeder steers], located in [county], [state]. [Yard or premises name, brands or other identifying detail.] Payment obligations: As a condition of releasing our security interest, payment for these farm products must be made by check or draft payable jointly to the debtor and the secured party [or: remitted directly to the secured party at the address above]. This notice expires on [date] unless renewed, or earlier if we send you a signed notice that it has lapsed. We will amend it in writing within three months of any material change. [Secured party] By: ______________________ [name, title] [phone]
