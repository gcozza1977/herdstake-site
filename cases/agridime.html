What happened

Agridime, a Texas-based company, sold cattle contracts: an investor bought a head of cattle, typically for $2,000, and Agridime promised to feed it and buy it back after about a year at a price 15% to 32% higher. The SEC alleged it raised at least $191 million from more than 2,100 investors in at least 15 states and used new money to pay earlier investors.

After a court put the company into receivership in late 2023, the numbers came apart. According to DTN, its books carried $59.18 million in cattle, which the receiver said would have meant more than 29,500 head. Its last monthly inventory email had reported 17,852 head at 18 feedyards in Colorado, Kansas, Illinois and Oklahoma. The receiver found about 9,900 head, and more than 2,000 of those belonged to others under a retained-ownership program.

A court later confirmed it had operated as a Ponzi scheme. A federal court ordered $103 million in restitution in the CFTC's case, and the SEC obtained a final judgment against the company and its founders.

What caught it

Regulators, then a receiver's count. One herd, three head counts: what the books implied, what the company told investors, and what was actually there.

Which check would have caught it sooner

Spread over 18 yards in four states, no single yard was over capacity, so a capacity check would not have flagged it.

What would have: each yard confirming the head on hand for the company's account, or a dated image of each lot, set against the contracts sold. The gap between 17,852 and about 9,900 sat in the yards' own records.
