What happened

A vice president at Community First Bank in Butler, Missouri, who was also a former pastor, pleaded guilty in September 2026 to wire fraud, bank fraud, aggravated identity theft and making false statements to a financial institution, according to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Western District of Missouri.

Prosecutors say that from April 2025 until the bank terminated him in February 2026, he used his position and standing to take at least $9.3 million from individuals and the bank in a fake cattle-buying scheme. Investors in his supposed cattle deals took out loans at the bank with him as loan officer and handed him the proceeds, without the bank knowing he benefited, and he never bought cattle with the money. He was the loan officer on about 29 loans and credit lines totaling about $4.48 million. In February 2026 he opened at least three loans in other people's names, using stolen identities, to pay off earlier ones.

What caught it

The public record doesn't say how it was first spotted. It ended with his termination and federal charges.

Which check would have caught it sooner

Here the loan officer was the control. Cattle loans that name no yard, or a yard with no record of the borrower's cattle, are the gap.

A screen run outside the loan officer's hands, tying each cattle loan to a named yard and a head count the yard can confirm, doesn't depend on the person who made the loan.
