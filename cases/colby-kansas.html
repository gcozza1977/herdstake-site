Colby, Kansas bank loans

Three loans to buy cattle that would secure them. Part of the money paid other debts.

What happened

According to the U.S. Attorney’s release, the borrower admitted applying for three loans from Farmers and Merchants Bank of Colby, Kansas, falsely representing that all of the money would be used to buy cattle that would serve as collateral. In fact some of it paid off existing debts and went into a political campaign account. The borrower pleaded guilty to one count of bank fraud and was sentenced on April 20, 2015 to 18 months, with $461,000 in restitution.

What caught it

The release read for this page does not say what first exposed it.

Which check would have caught it sooner

The collateral was never bought. A loan to purchase cattle is secured by nothing until the cattle exist.

What would have helped: paying the seller directly, or requiring the bill of sale, the inspection record and a head count before the next advance.
