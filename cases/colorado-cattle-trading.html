Ghost cattle case file

Cattle-trading Ponzi scheme

Investors were told their money was in short-term cattle trades. Earlier investors were paid with later investors’ money.

What happened

According to court documents summarized by the FDIC Office of Inspector General, from late 2017 until early 2019 two defendants and a co-conspirator ran a Ponzi scheme, representing to victims that their money was backed by short-term investments in cattle and marijuana businesses. In fact the money was used to pay earlier investors. A federal jury convicted the two in August 2022; on February 17, 2023 each was sentenced to six years, with $14,597,335.80 in restitution ordered jointly and severally.

The Securities and Exchange Commission had sued in Denver on September 30, 2019, naming among others Custom Consulting and Product Services, LLC; MR Cattle Production Services, LLC; Universal Herbs, LLC; DBC Limited, LLC; RM Farm and Livestock, LLC; and Sunshine Enterprises. The complaint alleged that investments were for the purported purchase and immediate resale of large numbers of cattle, that invoices and emails reflected fictitious cattle trades, and that at the height of the scheme more than $140 million a month moved through accounts under its organizer’s control.

What caught it

Which check would have caught it sooner

An invoice is not a head of cattle. Trades that are bought and resold at once leave nothing in a pen to look at unless someone asks for it.

What would have helped: checking each large trade against records that the seller does not write: where the cattle were, who inspected them, and whether the yard named could hold that many.
