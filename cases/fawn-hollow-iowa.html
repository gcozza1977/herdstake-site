Ghost cattle case file

Fawn Hollow, Iowa

A custom feeding operation sold cattle that belonged to its investors, in its operator’s own name, and kept the money.

What happened

According to the release, a large farming operation in northeast Iowa known as Fawn Hollow fed cattle owned by others, including investors across the United States, and sold the cattle, mainly to a Wisconsin slaughterhouse. Between July 2020 and February 2022 the proceeds of sales of cattle owned by eight investors, over $2.5 million, were converted. The investors had been persuaded to let the operator sell the cattle in the operator’s own name, and the slaughterhouse was told the seller had good title.

The operator pleaded guilty on December 1, 2023 to theft of livestock, wire fraud and a false bankruptcy declaration, and was sentenced on October 10, 2024 to more than 15 years. Restitution of over $5.7 million covers victims of the cattle theft and of other offenses described in the same case. Neither the operator nor Fawn Hollow was registered with USDA as a dealer under the Packers and Stockyards Act.

Which check would have caught it sooner

The cattle were real. The title was the problem. When the feeder sells in its own name, the owner’s interest does not show on the sale.

What would have helped: a lender or owner checking whether the operator is a registered, bonded dealer, who is named on the sale paperwork, and whether lien notice reached the buyer.
