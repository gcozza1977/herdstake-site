Ghost cattle case file

Independence, Kansas bank loan

A bank loan secured by a cattle count that was grossly overstated, and by cattle another lender had already financed.

Sentence21 monthsfederal prison, December 2013
What happened

According to the U.S. Attorney for the District of Kansas, on February 6, 2012 a borrower obtained a loan from First National Bank in Independence, Kansas, identifying cattle as collateral. The security agreement grossly overstated the number of cattle the borrower had.

The security agreement also listed as the borrower's own a group of Angus cattle that had been bought by a family member with a loan from USDA's Farm Service Agency (FSA). From September 2011 to March 2012 those FSA-financed cattle were sold, the FSA lien was concealed, and nothing from the sales was paid to FSA. By spring 2012 all of them had been sold, so the bank's loan had far less collateral behind it than the agreement showed.

The borrower pleaded guilty in federal court in Wichita to one count of loan fraud and one count of criminal conversion and was sentenced in December 2013 to 21 months in federal prison. The release gives no dollar loss and no head counts.

What caught it

The public release does not say. It credits USDA and the U.S. Attorney's office with the case.

Which check would have caught it sooner

Two lenders, one herd. The same cattle stood behind an FSA loan and, on paper, a bank loan. A search for other lenders' liens on the borrower's livestock, and for liens naming close family members on the same operation, is the first thing that would have shown it.

Then the count. A claimed head count checked against the permitted capacity of the place the cattle are said to be, and a dated record of where they are, makes a "grossly overstated" number visible before the loan closes.
