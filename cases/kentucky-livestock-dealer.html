What happened

According to the USDA Office of Inspector General, four people who operated or were associated with one of the largest livestock dealers in the United States orchestrated a check kiting and bank fraud scheme in 2009–2010, buying cattle from Kentucky producers with essentially non-existent funds. In June 2012 a Kentucky state court sentenced two to one year each, with $680,000 and $210,000 in restitution. The other two were sentenced to 10 years each and were also being prosecuted in federal court. The restitution figure here is only the two state orders, not the whole loss.

What caught it

The bulletin does not say what first exposed it.

Which check would have caught it sooner

The cattle moved; the money did not. A seller is exposed from the day cattle leave until the check clears.

What would have helped: checking the dealer’s bond and registration, and requiring prompt, cleared payment rather than relying on size and reputation.
