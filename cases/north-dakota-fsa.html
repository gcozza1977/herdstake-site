Ghost cattle case file

North Dakota FSA collateral

Cattle pledged for a Farm Service Agency operating loan were sold without the agency knowing.

What happened

The USDA Office of Inspector General reports that a producer converted cattle pledged as security for an FSA operating loan by selling, without FSA knowledge, 44 head secured in the producer’s name and 94 head secured in a brother’s name, with the proceeds moved back to the producer. On January 10, 2014, in federal court in North Dakota, the producer pleaded guilty and was sentenced to two years of probation, 50 hours of community service and $85,600 in restitution.

What caught it

The bulletin does not say what first exposed it.

Which check would have caught it sooner

Collateral in a second name is easy to miss at the sale barn.

What would have helped: filings and buyer notices covering every name the pledged cattle can be sold under, and a periodic count against the security agreement.
