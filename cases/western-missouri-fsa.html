Ghost cattle case file

Western Missouri FSA loans

Cattle pledged to the Farm Service Agency were sold, and other owners’ cattle were retagged and commingled.

What happened

The USDA Office of Inspector General reports a three-part scheme causing a loss of almost $300,000. The farmer, who had FSA annual and term operating loans, sold at least 114 head of FSA-mortgaged cattle valued at about $138,000; removed ear tags from 646 head owned by others and placed there to graze, commingled them and sold $124,000 worth of cattle belonging to others; and told an insurer that cattle had drowned or died, receiving $31,000.

What caught it

A referral from the Farm Service Agency: the loans were delinquent, and it appeared collateral may have been sold without the agency’s consent.

Which check would have caught it sooner

Tags can be removed. A head count that relies on tags alone does not show whose cattle are standing there.

What would have helped: a count and brand or ownership check while the loans were current, compared with sale records.
