Ghost cattle case file

Wood County, Texas cattle loans

Cattle pledged on several bank loans. After foreclosure, the cattle were not returned.

What happened

According to the Texas and Southwestern Cattle Raisers Association (TSCRA), whose Special Rangers investigated the case with the lending bank, a borrower took out several agricultural loans using cattle as collateral. The loans went into default in June and July 2024. After the bank foreclosed, the borrower did not turn over the cattle or repay the principal and interest.

A Wood County grand jury indicted the borrower on March 30, 2026 on two state felony counts of hindering a secured creditor, one of them in the first-degree tier for more than $300,000. On November 5, 2026, in the 402nd Judicial District Court, the borrower pleaded guilty to both counts and received 10 years of deferred adjudication community supervision, with $439,330.07 in restitution: $150,000 paid upfront and the rest in installments over 10 years.

What caught it

The defaults, then foreclosure. The bank went to collect its collateral, the cattle were not delivered, and the bank and TSCRA's Special Rangers worked out what had happened.

Which check would have caught it sooner

This is a small case, and that is the point. Most cattle collateral losses are not nine-figure frauds; they are a few loans where the lender learns where the cattle are only when it tries to take them.

What would have helped: knowing, before the default, where each pledged herd was kept and how many head were there, with a dated record of it, so a missing herd shows up while the loan is still current rather than after foreclosure.
