Questions

What lenders ask first.

Can satellites really count cattle in a packed pen?

Not head by head with perfect accuracy. That's why every report gives a range and the image date, and why a claim above the range gets a closer look and, where warranted, a physical count, not an accusation. The frauds this is built to catch weren't close calls: in the cases with published counts, most of the claimed cattle weren't there.

Can a satellite tell whose cattle they are?

No. An image shows cattle in pens on one date. It can't show who owns them, whether they're pledged to another lender, or whether they were sold. That's why each check ties your borrower's claim to the yard's pen records and the lien record, and treats the image as one piece of evidence.

How accurate are you?

We're measuring it now in a blind test against five feedyards' own pen sheets, with our counts fixed before we see theirs. Our target is within about 5%. Pilot lenders get the results, misses included.

Does this replace our site visits?

No. It's a documented check between visits that tells you which borrowers deserve the next visit first.

What do you need from us, and who sees the reports?

A short list: borrower, yard and claimed head, plus any yard inventory statements you already receive. No system access and no credit files. Reports go only to you, never to another lender or the yard.

Is this a guarantee or an appraisal?

Neither. It's a monitoring report: we report head and location, and you apply your own price.
