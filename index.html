Questions

What lenders ask first.

Can satellites really count cattle in a packed pen?

Not head by head with perfect accuracy. That's why every report gives a range with a 90% confidence level and the image date, and why a claim above the range gets a physical count rather than an accusation. The frauds this is built to catch weren't close calls: in the three cases with published counts, between 66% and 95% of the claimed cattle weren't there.

How accurate are you?

We're measuring it now in a blind test against five feedyards' own pen sheets, with our counts fixed before we see theirs. Our target is within about 5%. The results, misses included, go on our method page as they come in.

Does this replace our site visits?

No. It's a documented check between visits that tells you which borrowers deserve the next visit first. We still recommend a physical count on every flag and at least once a year.

Who reviews and signs the reports?

Our collateral review lead, who has at least five years in agricultural lending, ag bank examination or livestock collateral inspection. No report reaches you without that review and signature.

What do you need from us?

A short list: borrower, yard and claimed head. Nothing else, and no access to your systems.

Who sees our reports?

Only you. Reports are for the ordering lender's own use and never go to another lender, a yard or anyone else. If a borrower disputes a finding, we work through you.

Which states do you cover?

Texas, Kansas and Nebraska today. Tell us where your cattle book sits and we'll tell you when we reach it.

Is this a guarantee or an appraisal?

Neither. It's a monitoring report. We report head and location, and you apply your own price. It flags gaps early so you can act.
