Lien rules by state · Alabama

Alabama cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Alabama among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "Specified"). The Secretary of State is the system operator under Administrative Code chapter 820-4-2, Farm Products.

System

Central filing. A lender files an effective financing statement (EFS) with the Secretary of State on the Alabama UCC-1F form; amendments, continuations and terminations go on the UCC-3F. The statement names the debtor, with a Social Security or tax identification number, the secured party, the farm product and its code (cattle and calves are code 201, and miscellaneous categories are not allowed), the crop year unless every crop is covered, and the debtor's and secured party's signatures (rule 820-4-2-.03). Only products produced in Alabama can be registered. If no county or amount is shown, all of that product the debtor owns in Alabama is covered (.03(11)-(12)). An amendment is due within three months of a material change, and the statement is effective five years, continued by a filing in the six months before it expires (.03(4)-(5)). The Secretary of State indexes filings into a monthly master list by farm product, debtor, crop year and county (.04, .05).

What a buyer must do

A buyer, commission merchant or selling agent can register with the Secretary of State for a year, per farm product and optionally per county, on form UCC-20, and receives the master list monthly by electronic means or mail (rule 820-4-2-.05). The rule states the consequence of not registering: such a person, inside or outside Alabama, is subject to the security interests on the master list whether or not it knows of them, so it needs to query the Secretary of State about any seller of an Alabama farm product (.07(3)). The Secretary of State gives oral confirmation of a filing within 24 hours of a telephone request, by debtor name, tax number or filing number, followed by written confirmation (.05(9)). The Secretary of State adopts the federal rule, 9 CFR Part 205, by reference (.07(2)); the statute is 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

The rule sets a $5.00 charge for certifying a copy plus $1.00 a page, a $9.75 access fee per electronic farm product transaction on top of any contract provider fee, and an extra $5.00 when a non-standard form is used instead of the UCC-1F or UCC-3F (rule 820-4-2-.03, .07(4)). Filing, registration and written confirmation fees are set by the Code of Alabama (the rule points to Code of Ala. 1975 § 7-9A-525), which We did not read, so those amounts are not given here. Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State.

Brand inspection

Not confirmed. We did not read an Alabama statute or rule on cattle brand inspection at an official source, so the page does not say whether one is required. Ask the Alabama Department of Agriculture and Industries before relying on this.
