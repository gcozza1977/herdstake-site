Lien rules by state · Arizona

Arizona cattle lien rules.

Direct notice, with mandatory brand inspection before cattle are sold or moved, subject to the statute's exceptions.

System

Direct notice, per the 19-state USDA list on our index page, which does not include Arizona. USDA refused our request on November 25, 2026, so the list was not re-read.

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle in the ordinary course takes free of the lender's lien unless, within 1 year before the sale, it received written notice meeting 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1) and then failed to meet the payment terms in it. Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g).

Arizona's UCC rule for buyers

A.R.S. § 47-9320(A) leaves buyers of farm products from a person engaged in farming operations out of the usual buyer-takes-free rule. The section as posted adds no farm-products notice rules of its own.

Where financing statements are filed

For cattle, the filing office is the Arizona Secretary of State (A.R.S. § 47-9501(A)(2)). Search method and fees: not yet confirmed by Herdstake.

Brand inspection

Livestock (other than equines and swine) may not be slaughtered, sold, purchased, driven or shipped until a livestock officer or inspector has inspected them for health, brands and marks and the fee is paid, with exceptions in the statute, including approved self-inspection by movers, feedlots and dairies (A.R.S. §§ 3-1336, 3-1337). Cattle sold at auction are inspected out on an inspection certificate or a validated auction invoice. The statute sets a $3 service charge plus 25 cents a head for cattle. Hauling livestock without the inspection record, auction invoice, registration or identification card, or bill of sale is a class 2 misdemeanor, subject to the article's exceptions (§ 3-1341). Check with the Department of Agriculture for current charges and any other amounts collected at inspection.
