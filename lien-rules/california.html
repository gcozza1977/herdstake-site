Lien rules by state · California

California cattle lien rules.

Direct notice. California is not on USDA's list of 19 states with a certified central filing system, so a lender protects itself against cattle buyers by sending them written notice under the federal Food Security Act.

System

Direct notice. California is not on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (the list names 19 states). There is no state register of effective financing statements for buyers to check, so the federal direct-notice rule in 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1) applies.

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle in the ordinary course takes free of the lender's lien unless, within 1 year before the sale, it received the lender's or seller's written notice meeting 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1) and then failed to meet the payment terms in it (such as a check payable jointly to the borrower and the lender). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). California's version of UCC § 9-320(a) (Cal. Com. Code § 9320(a)) does not carry the uniform text's carve-out for buyers of farm products from a person engaged in farming operations; for cattle sold in the ordinary course, the federal rule in § 1631 is the one to work from.

Searching financing statements

The California Secretary of State is the filing office for UCC financing statements. Filings and debtor searches are made online through the Secretary of State (bizfile Online; the UCC page also points to UCC Connect), or on paper forms UCC1 and UCC3 by mail or at the Sacramento counter. The Secretary of State's fee list shows: UCC-1 or UCC-3 filing $5 online, $10 on paper for 2 pages or less and $20 for 3 pages or more; standard debtor name search $5 online (waived if a certificate is ordered at the time of the search); debtor search certificate $5 online or $10 on paper (form UCC11); image view $1 a document; copies $1 for the first page and $0.50 for each additional page; certification $5. Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Yes. The California Department of Food and Agriculture's Bureau of Livestock Identification says cattle must be brand inspected when they are sold or change ownership, before they leave the state, on entry into a registered feedlot, before release from a public saleyard or cattle sales market, before slaughter, before leaving a designated point-of-origin inspection area, and before moving from quarantined premises (Food and Agricultural Code § 21051, with §§ 21111, 21141 and 9565). A brand inspection certificate travels with cattle when inspection is required; for moves within the state that need no inspection, a bill of sale or consignment ("yellow slip") goes with them. CDFA's notice of fees effective January 1, 2024 lists $1.60 a head for most inspections (ranch, change of ownership, out-of-state sale or pasture, salesyard and slaughter), lower amounts for some feedlot moves, and a service charge of $25 per site for 29 head or fewer or $15 per site for 30 head or more (not charged at salesyards, feedlots, slaughter plants or fairs). Confirm current fees with the Bureau.
