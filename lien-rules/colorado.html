Lien rules by state · Colorado

Colorado cattle lien rules.

Central filing. Colorado is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All"), run by the Secretary of State.

System

Central filing. Colorado's central filing officer is the Secretary of State (C.R.S. 4-9.5-103), and the system is certified under the Food Security Act of 1985. The law is C.R.S. Title 4, Article 9.5, "Central Filing of Effective Financing Statements" (§§ 4-9.5-101 to 4-9.5-113).

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle takes subject to the lender's lien if it failed to register with the state's central filing system before buying and the lender had filed an effective financing statement (EFS) covering the cattle (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2)), or if it received the state's written notice naming the seller and product and did not secure a waiver or release (§ 1631(e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). An EFS does not replace the lender's UCC financing statement. Under C.R.S. 4-9.5-104.5, buyers, commission merchants and selling agents may register, on an annual basis. The Secretary of State says its master list is updated monthly, current through the last day of the prior month, with update emails usually sent on the 1st of each month. To get them, a buyer creates a Secretary of State account and registers for the master list under "Manage EFS master list registration". The Secretary of State's pages list no registration fee; confirm with the office.

Searching financing statements

All UCC filings in Colorado are made online with the Secretary of State. Searches and copies are free on its website (coloradosos.gov/ucc), and searching or downloading the master list is free and needs no account. Posted filing fees: UCC financing statement $8; effective financing statement $8; amendment $8; debtor lien search, master list search and certified search free.

Brand inspection

Colorado requires a brand inspection by the Department of Agriculture's Brand Inspection Division before ownership of livestock changes (including when livestock are given away), before transport of more than 75 miles within Colorado, and before transport out of state. Cattle fees in effect since October 1, 2025: $1.00 per head for a country inspection or at a sale barn; $0.98 per head feedlot to slaughter (under 500 head) or $0.95 (over 500); $0.83 certified feedlot to slaughter; $40 minimum per stop.
