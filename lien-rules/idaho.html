Lien rules by state · Idaho

Idaho cattle lien rules.

Central filing, with a question for cattle. Idaho is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (certified September 23, 1986; products covered: "Specified"), run by the Secretary of State, but the product codes it publishes list crops and hay, not livestock.

System

Central filing. USDA lists Idaho as certified on September 23, 1986, last amended July 15, 2014, with products covered shown as "Specified"; USDA's page does not list which products. The Secretary of State runs the Idaho Central Filing System: farm-product financing statements (UCC-1F) go on a master list that is published every two weeks (Idaho Code title 28, chapter 9, and title 45, chapter 18; IDAPA 34.05.01). The Secretary of State's published list of farm product codes names only crops, hay and ensilage. We found no cattle or livestock code on it, so ask the Secretary of State, and counsel, whether a filing covering cattle is on the master list or whether a lender should also send direct notice (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1)).

What a buyer must do

Idaho's UCC leaves a person buying farm products from a person engaged in farming operations out of its ordinary buyer rule (Idaho Code § 28-9-320(a)). Under that same subsection, such a buyer takes free of a security interest only if it registered with the Secretary of State (§ 28-9-523(h)) and the interest is not on the most recent master list or cumulative supplement (§ 28-9-523(i)); written notice of the interest from the Secretary of State, the seller or the lender defeats that protection. The federal rule for central filing states is the same in substance (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2) and (e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). To receive the master list, a buyer subscribes with the Secretary of State: $60 a year, which includes the seed and labor list.

Searching financing statements

Search online at the Secretary of State's SOSbiz UCC search. The Secretary of State lists a non-certified UCC search at $3 per search; certified searches are ordered as copies through SOSbiz. A UCC-1F filed electronically costs $10, or $30 on the fillable PDF form, plus $1 per extra page for print and mail, and paper forms carry a $20 manual processing fee. Confirm current fees on the Secretary of State's UCC page before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Yes, statewide (Idaho Code title 25, chapter 11; § 25-1120, Brand inspection). The Idaho State Police brand inspector says cattle must be inspected whenever ownership changes in any manner, before leaving Idaho and before slaughter; an inspection for leaving the state or for slaughter is good for 96 hours. When a seller gives a bill of sale instead, the buyer must get and pay for a brand inspection within ten days, and the state's advice to buyers is to require the seller's brand inspection certificate. Fees on the state's fee page (last updated July 1, 2025): $3.12 per head of cattle for a regular inspection ($1.35 inspection, $1.50 Idaho Beef Council, $0.22 animal disease control, $0.05 predator control), $7.77 per head for an annual or seasonal transportation inspection, a $30 minimum when an inspector travels and a $100 auction minimum. A newer FY2027 fee schedule is posted as a PDF we could not read, so confirm the fee with the brand office. Recording a brand costs $75 to $235.

State offices

Idaho Secretary of State, UCC and central filing (208-334-3191) · Idaho State Police, State Brand Inspector (208-884-7070)
