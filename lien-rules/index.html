The federal rule

When a cattle buyer takes free of the lender's lien

Under the Food Security Act of 1985 (7 U.S.C. § 1631(d)), a buyer who buys farm products in the ordinary course of business from a seller engaged in farming takes free of a security interest the seller created, even if the interest is perfected and the buyer knows of it. The exceptions are in § 1631(e):

  • Direct-notice states (§ 1631(e)(1)): the buyer takes subject to the lien if, within 1 year before the sale, it received written notice from the secured party or the seller with the contents the statute lists, and then failed to perform the payment obligations in that notice.
  • States with a USDA-certified central filing system (§ 1631(e)(2) and (e)(3)): the buyer takes subject to the lien if it failed to register with the state's system before buying and the lender had filed an effective financing statement covering those products, or if it received the state's written notice naming the seller and product and did not secure a waiver or release, for example by paying as required.

Commission merchants and selling agents, such as sale barns selling cattle for others, follow parallel rules in § 1631(g). Whether a notice was received is decided by the law of the state where the buyer resides (§ 1631(f) and (g)(3)).

Which system applies

The 19 states with a certified central filing system

USDA's Agricultural Marketing Service lists these 19 states as having central filing systems certified under the Food Security Act. Every other state, including Texas and Kansas, is a direct-notice state. "Products covered" is shown exactly as USDA prints it; USDA's page does not define "Specified" or the asterisk, so check the state's certification before relying on it for cattle.
