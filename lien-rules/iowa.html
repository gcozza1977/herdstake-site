Lien rules by state · Iowa

Iowa cattle lien rules.

Direct notice. Iowa is not on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system, so lenders protect their lien by giving buyers written notice under the Food Security Act.

System

Direct notice. Iowa does not appear among the 19 states on USDA's list of certified central filing systems, so the federal direct-notice rule applies (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1)). Iowa Code § 554.9102(4) says when that written notice counts as received for the Food Security Act: when it is delivered in hand, or mailed by certified or registered mail; a refusal to accept a notice mailed that way counts as receipt.

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle takes free of the lender's security interest unless, within one year before the sale, it received written notice from the lender (or the seller) that meets § 1631(e)(1) and did not then make payment as the notice directs, for example jointly to the seller and the lender (§ 1631(e)(1)(B)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). Iowa's UCC (Iowa Code § 554.9320) leaves buyers of farm products from farmers out of its ordinary buyer rule, so the federal rule governs. Iowa also gives a perfected purchase-money security interest in livestock priority over an earlier interest in the same livestock if the earlier secured party gets signed notice in time (Iowa Code § 554.9324(4)).

Liens that can come ahead of the lender

Iowa has several statutory liens on cattle that, once perfected, can outrank an earlier lender. An agricultural supply dealer that sold feed has a lien on the livestock that ate it (Iowa Code § 570A.3), perfected by a financing statement with the Secretary of State within 31 days after the farmer buys (§ 570A.4); its priority over an earlier lien is limited to the difference set by § 570A.5, and a dealer can send the lender a certified request first (§ 570A.2). A custom cattle feedlot has a lien perfected by filing within 20 days after the cattle arrive, superior to earlier-perfected interests with narrow exceptions (§ 579A.2), and after the cattle leave it can reach identifiable cash proceeds or a processor that bought them within three days (§ 579A.3). A contract producer under a commodity production contract has a lien filed within 45 days after the livestock arrive (§ 579B.4). A veterinarian's lien filed within 60 days of treatment has priority regardless of when other interests were perfected (§§ 581.2, 581.3). Search for these as well as the lender's own UCC filing.

Searching financing statements

Financing statements on livestock and farm products are filed with the Iowa Secretary of State (Iowa Code § 554.9501). The Secretary of State lists a lien search at $5 per debtor, a UCC-1 at $10 for 1 to 2 pages ($20 for 3 or more), $5 per filing online through Fast Track Filing, copies at $1 per page and certified copies at $3 plus the copy charge. Confirm current fees on the Secretary of State's UCC page before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Branding is optional in Iowa (Iowa Code § 169A.2), but a brand used must be recorded with the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship: $25 per location, renewed every five years for $25 (§§ 169A.4, 169A.13), $15 to record a transfer. Neither chapter 169A nor the department's brand page mentions a brand inspection for cattle sales; we found no state brand-inspection requirement in the sources we read.
