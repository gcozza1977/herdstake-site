Lien rules by state · Louisiana

Louisiana cattle lien rules.

Central filing. Louisiana is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system.

System

Central filing. Louisiana is on the list of 19 states USDA's Agricultural Marketing Service gives as having a certified central filing system, with products covered shown as "All" (list as Herdstake read it for the index page; USDA's page refused our request on November 25, 2026, so it was not re-read). The Secretary of State keeps the central registry, a master index of all effective financing statements (R.S. 3:3652 and 3:3654).

What a buyer must do

Under 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2) and (e)(3), a buyer takes subject to the lien if it did not register with the state's central filing system before buying and the lender had filed an effective financing statement covering the product, or if it received the state's written notice naming the seller and product and did not get a waiver or release, for example by paying as required. Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). Herdstake has not confirmed the steps for registering as a buyer in Louisiana: ask the Secretary of State before buying.

Searching financing statements

Lenders file effective financing statements with the clerk of court of any parish, not with the Secretary of State directly; the clerk sends the data to the central registry (R.S. 3:3656). Anyone may request an encumbrance certificate from a filing officer (R.S. 3:3654(D)); the statute sets the fee at $15 (R.S. 3:3657). Other registry access fees are set by rule and not yet confirmed by Herdstake.

Brand inspection

No livestock may be sold at a public sale until the seller has offered it for inspection to a brand inspector or a designee of the Livestock Brand Commission (R.S. 3:742). Brand inspectors may also inspect livestock being transported. The Commission keeps the brand records and publishes a brand book.
