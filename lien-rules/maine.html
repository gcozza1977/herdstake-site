Lien rules by state · Maine

Maine cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Maine among the states with a certified central filing system, certified December 2, 1986 (products covered: "All"). State law lets the Secretary of State run it and set its rules and fees (5 M.R.S. § 90-A).

System

Central filing. Maine law allows the Secretary of State to establish and operate a central filing system that records and gives notice of financing statements covering security interests in farm products, to adopt rules for it, and to set a schedule of fees for filing, registering, notices and related services (5 M.R.S. § 90-A). USDA certified Maine's system on December 2, 1986, for all farm products.

What a buyer must do

Under the federal rule for central filing states, a buyer of farm products takes them subject to the lender's security interest if the buyer did not register with the Secretary of State before the purchase and the lender has filed an effective financing statement covering the products sold (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2)). A registered buyer also takes subject to it if the Secretary of State's written notice names both the seller and the farm product as covered by an effective financing statement and the buyer does not get a waiver or release from the lender, for example by meeting the payment terms in that notice (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(3)). For a registered buyer who receives the master list, Maine treats the notice as received on the date the Secretary of State sent or delivered the list (5 M.R.S. § 90-A). A buyer who gets a direct written notice from the lender or seller within a year before the sale takes subject to the interest if it does not meet the payment terms in that notice (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1)).

Searching and fees

Financing statements are searched and filed with the Secretary of State's Uniform Commercial Code office, which has an online search and filing service. Its printed fee schedule lists an official search at $12.00 online or $20.00 by written request ($5.00 more for a certified search), and a UCC-1 financing statement at $10.00 filed electronically or $15.00 on paper for one or two pages. The schedule we read does not list a fee for registering as a buyer to receive the farm products master list, so that fee is not confirmed: ask the Secretary of State.

Brand inspection

Not confirmed. We did not read a Maine statute or rule on cattle brand inspection at an official source, so this page does not say whether one is required. Ask the Maine Department of Agriculture, Conservation and Forestry before relying on this.
