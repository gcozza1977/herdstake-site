Lien rules by state · Michigan

Michigan cattle lien rules.

Direct notice, with Michigan's own potential-buyer list and joint-check rules on top.

System

Direct notice, per the 19-state USDA list on our index page, which does not include Michigan. USDA refused our request on November 25, 2026, so the list was not re-read.

The federal rule

A buyer of cattle in the ordinary course takes free of the lender's lien unless, within 1 year before the sale, it received written notice meeting 7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(1) and then failed to meet the payment terms in it. Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g).

What Michigan's own statute adds (MCL 440.9320)

If the lender asks, the farmer must list potential buyers and delivery points, no more than 5 buyers per commodity, and may not sell to a buyer off the list without the lender's written consent. The lender may send listed buyers written notice, by certified mail or another method with verifiable receipt. A buyer who has received that notice must pay by check payable jointly to the seller and the lender, unless the lender authorizes in writing payment directly to it; violating this is a misdemeanor (up to 90 days, a fine up to $1,000, or both). Payment of the debt before prosecution starts is a defense. The section also covers buyers who set off their own debts, and commission merchants and selling agents: read subsections (13) to (16).

Where financing statements are filed

For cattle, the filing office is the Michigan Secretary of State (MCL 440.9501(1)(b)). The statute sets the fee for a search of the records concerning a debtor at $6.00, plus $2.00 per page if you ask for copies, $25.00 more to expedite, and $6.00 more for the Secretary of State's seal on the certificate (MCL 440.9525(2)). Confirm the current fee before you send payment. Search method: not yet confirmed by Herdstake.
