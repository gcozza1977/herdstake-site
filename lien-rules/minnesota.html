Lien rules by state · Minnesota

Minnesota cattle lien rules.

Central filing. Minnesota is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (certified June 29, 1993, amended May 1, 2006; products covered: "Specified"), run by the Secretary of State. Cattle and calves are on the statute's product list.

System

Central filing. USDA lists Minnesota as certified on June 29, 1993 and amended May 1, 2006, with products covered shown as "Specified". The Secretary of State runs the computerized filing system under Minn. Stat. ch. 336A (336A.01, 336A.04). The statute's product list includes cattle and calves, along with bison, hogs, sheep and other livestock (336A.02), and for animals the "crop year" is the calendar year they are born or acquired (336A.01). An effective financing statement (EFS) is a separate filing from the UCC financing statement and may not be combined with it (336A.03); it states the amount by head or similar units, never in dollars, and is effective for 5 years, renewable (336A.06). Filing an EFS costs $20 through the Secretary of State's website or $25 by other means (336A.04).

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle takes subject to the lender's lien if it failed to register with the state's central filing system before buying and the lender had filed an EFS covering the cattle (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2)), or if it received the state's written notice naming the seller and product and did not secure a waiver or release, for example by paying the seller and lender jointly (§ 1631(e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). Minnesota's UCC (Minn. Stat. 336.9-320) leaves buyers of farm products from farmers out of its ordinary buyer rule. Buyers register with the Secretary of State for 12 months at $25 a year (336A.11); registered buyers receive the master list monthly, by the tenth of each month, free electronically or $250 to $400 a year on paper, and may ask for an "all livestock" list (336A.08). Farm-product statutory liens follow the same buyer rule (336A.15, 336A.16). The revisor notes that some 2014 amendments to these sections take effect only after USDA approves a Minnesota proposal; we could not confirm whether that has happened, so confirm the current registration steps with the Secretary of State.

Liens that can outrank a lender

Minn. Stat. 514.966 gives agricultural liens on livestock, each perfected by filing a UCC financing statement. Once perfected, an emergency veterinarian's lien (filed within 180 days) comes first; a feeder's lien for feeding, pasture or care (filed within 60 days) is next; then a breeder's lien. A livestock production input lien for feed or labor can also outrank the lender's security interest if the supplier sends the lender a lien-notification statement by certified mail and the lender does not answer within 10 calendar days with a commitment or a written refusal; it is capped by the gain in the animals' value (514.965, 514.966).

Searching financing statements

Search and file at the Secretary of State's business liens pages, which offer separate online searches for UCC filings and for central notification (CNS) filings. By statute a CNS search with report costs $20 per debtor name (336A.09) and a UCC information request, including a certificate on whether any financing statement names a debtor, costs $20 (336.9-525). The Secretary of State's own fee table could not be read on the date checked.

Brands

No brand inspection on sale. Brand registration with the Board of Animal Health is voluntary: $10 for a 10-year registration, $10 to reregister or record a transfer (Minn. Stat. 35.824, 35.826, 35.829). A registered brand is prima facie evidence of ownership (35.828). The former bill-of-sale rule for branded animals (35.830) was repealed in 2025.

State offices

Minnesota Secretary of State, business liens (UCC and CNS) · Minnesota Board of Animal Health, brands
