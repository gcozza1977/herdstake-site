Lien rules by state · Mississippi

Mississippi cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Mississippi among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All"). The Secretary of State, Division of Business Services, runs it under Mississippi Code § 75-9-320(g) and the Secretary of State's rule 604.

System

Central filing. A lender files its security interest in farm products on the Mississippi UCC-1F, the form the Secretary of State designates as the effective financing statement, with the Secretary of State's Division of Business Services (rule 604.3, 604.3.2). The filing must give the debtor's name, address and tax or Social Security number, the secured party, the crop year unless every crop is covered, the farm product name and code, each Mississippi county where it is produced, and the debtor's signature if filed on paper (rule 604.3.1). An amendment (UCC-3F) is due within three months of a material change and must be signed by both the person who subjects the product to the interest and the secured party; the statement stays effective five years and can be continued in the six months before it expires (rule 604.3.3). The Secretary of State compiles a master list by farm product, debtor and county (rule 604.6).

What a buyer must do

The Secretary of State's rule says the federal Food Security Act does not require a buyer, commission merchant or selling agent to register, but one that does not register is subject to the security interests on the state's master list whether or not it knows of them, so it needs to ask the Secretary of State about any seller of a product produced in Mississippi (rule 604.5.5). A buyer or agent can register for a year, per farm product and optionally per county, and choose how often to receive the list, at least monthly, in printed form or with online access (rule 604.5). A registered person is subject to a filing if it receives written notice of it, and notice is treated as received three days after mailing or on actual delivery if earlier (rule 604.3.4.2-604.3.4.3). On request the Secretary of State gives oral confirmation of a filing within 48 hours, followed in writing by the next business day (rule 604.5.6). These rules apply only to filings in Mississippi's system for products produced in Mississippi. The federal rule is in 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

The Secretary of State's fee schedule (revised October 2024) lists $10 for an initial farm product filing (UCC-1F with addendum), plus $3 if there are attachments and $4 for each additional debtor, and $10 for an amendment (UCC-3F), plus $3 for attachments; farm product filings must be paid for by mail. The schedule I read does not list a buyer registration or master list fee, so none is given here. Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State.

Brand inspection

Not confirmed. I did not find a Mississippi statewide brand inspection requirement at an official source. The Mississippi Board of Animal Health sets health-certificate and identification rules for cattle entering the state, which are separate from brand inspection. Ask the Board of Animal Health before relying on this.

State offices

Mississippi Secretary of State, Business Services (601-359-1350) · Mississippi Board of Animal Health
