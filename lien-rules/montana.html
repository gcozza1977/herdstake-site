Lien rules by state · Montana

Montana cattle lien rules.

Central filing, and cattle are covered. Montana is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (certified September 9, 1986; products covered: "All"), run by the Secretary of State, and its effective financing statements name cattle by product.

System

Central filing. USDA lists Montana as certified on September 9, 1986, with no amendment shown and products covered shown as "All". A lender files the effective financing statement (EFS) with the Secretary of State (Mont. Code Ann. § 30-9A-501(3)). The Secretary of State says "farm product" includes livestock such as cattle, hogs, sheep, horses and poultry. Montana uses no numeric product codes: its EFS instructions say to list the specific farm product, such as cattle, and that listing all "livestock and crops" will not do. For animals the crop year is the year born or acquired; with the quantity left blank, the EFS covers all of that product the debtor owns; an EFS lasts five years unless continued. The EFS protects only against buyers of the farm product, so a lender also files a regular UCC financing statement against other creditors.

What a buyer must do

Montana's UCC leaves a person buying farm products from a person engaged in farming operations out of its ordinary buyer rule (Mont. Code Ann. § 30-9A-320(1)), so the federal rule decides. In a central filing state, a buyer of cattle takes subject to the lender's interest if it did not register with the Secretary of State before buying and an EFS was filed, or if it received the Secretary of State's written notice naming the seller and the product and did not get a release from the lender (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2) and (e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). Buyers, commission merchants and selling agents register through the Secretary of State's business portal and choose the parts of the master list they want. Registration is free; the master list costs $20 a month online, updated daily.

Searching financing statements

Search and file online at the Secretary of State's Business Filing Portal (biz.sosmt.gov). The Secretary of State's UCC fee sheet (revised July 6, 2023) lists $7 per debtor-name search, $7 to file a UCC lien, an agricultural lien or an EFS, $5 for a continuation or other amendment, no fee for a termination, $5 for copies with a search, $7 for certified copies and $25 a month for a UCC search subscription. Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Yes, statewide and at county lines (Mont. Code Ann. title 81, chapter 3, part 2). Livestock may not be removed from a county, or change ownership by sale or otherwise, unless brand-inspected and a certificate issued, with a narrow exception for transfers within a family that stay on the same premises (§ 81-3-211(2)); inspection is also required before sale at a livestock market and before slaughter at a licensed plant (§ 81-3-211(3)), and before livestock leave the state (§ 81-3-213). The seller requests the inspection and pays the fee (§ 81-3-211(7)). The Department of Livestock lists cattle inspection at $1.00 per head; ownership is shown by a signed bill of sale or the most recent inspection. A 2025 rule notice (MAR 2025-303) proposed changes to permit fees and how long a preslaughter inspection is valid; confirm current rules with the Brands Enforcement Division.
