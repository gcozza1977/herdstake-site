Lien rules by state · New Hampshire

New Hampshire cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists New Hampshire among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All"). The Secretary of State runs it under the State Food Security Act, RSA chapter 439.

System

Central filing. A secured party files with the Secretary of State a signed form giving the borrower's name, address and signature, the secured party's name and address, the borrower's Social Security or tax identification number, a description of the farm products and the amount, and a description of the real estate, including the county, where they are located (RSA 439:2, I). The form must be amended in writing within three months of a material change, and its effectiveness, continuation and amendment are treated as if it were a financing statement filed under RSA 382-A:9 (RSA 439:2, II). The Secretary of State keeps a central index and a master list organized by farm product, then by borrower name, tax number, county and crop year (RSA 439:3, I).

What a buyer must do

A secured party can enforce its lien against a buyer who purchases farm products in the ordinary course from a farmer, or against a commission merchant or selling agent who sells them for a farmer, only if the buyer or agent received written notice of the lien that meets the federal Food Security Act, or the secured party filed the form with the Secretary of State described above (RSA 439:2, I). Buyers, commission merchants and selling agents can register with the Secretary of State to receive the parts of the master list they ask for, in written or printed form, on a schedule the Secretary of State sets (RSA 439:3, II-III). Anyone can ask the Secretary of State for oral confirmation of a filing within 24 hours, followed by written confirmation (RSA 439:3, IV). The federal rule is in 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

Registering as a buyer, commission merchant or selling agent costs $100 a year (RSA 439:3, II). The fee for filing and indexing the form, or a continuation or amendment, follows RSA 478:17-g, which sets $15 for the first recorded page plus $4 for each additional page for filings under RSA 439 (RSA 439:2, II; RSA 478:17-g, I). Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State.

Brand inspection

Not confirmed. We did not read a New Hampshire statute or rule on cattle brand inspection at an official source, so the page does not say whether one is required. Ask the Department of Agriculture, Markets and Food before relying on this.
