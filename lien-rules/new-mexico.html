Lien rules by state · New Mexico

New Mexico cattle lien rules.

Central filing. New Mexico is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (certified December 29, 1987; products covered: "Specified"), run by the Secretary of State under the state's Farm Products Secured Interest Act.

System

Central filing. USDA lists New Mexico as certified on December 29, 1987, with products covered shown as "Specified", so check that the product you lend against is one the system covers. The Secretary of State describes a statewide filing system in which a lender registers its security interest in farm products by filing an effective financing statement (EFS) (Farm Products Secured Interest Act, NMSA 1978 §§ 56-13-1 to 56-13-14; rule 12.7.2 NMAC). The EFS protects against buyers of the farm product; a lender also files a regular UCC financing statement against other creditors.

What a buyer must do

New Mexico's UCC leaves a person buying farm products from a person engaged in farming operations out of its ordinary buyer rule, and points to the Farm Products Secured Interest Act (NMSA 1978 § 55-9-320(a)), so the central filing rules decide. In a central filing state, a buyer of cattle takes subject to the lender's interest if it did not register with the Secretary of State before buying and an EFS was filed, or if it received the Secretary of State's written notice naming the seller and the product and did not get a release from the lender (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2) and (e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). Under 12.7.2 NMAC, buyer registration is $30 a year and the farm product list is $300 a year.

Searching financing statements

UCC filings are made online only, through the Secretary of State's portal (enterprise.sos.nm.gov), which also has a UCC search; paper UCC and EFS filings have not been accepted since March 2024. Rule 12.6.2 NMAC says there is no fee to use the online search engine and copies are $1.00 a page; the Secretary of State's online filing help (revised March 2024) lists $20 for a UCC-1 ($120 for a transmitting utility or manufactured home) and $20 for a UCC-3 amendment. Under 12.7.2 NMAC, an EFS filing, amendment, continuation or termination is $11.50 each ($0.50 per attached page), and a search of the farm products system is $15 per debtor name. Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Yes. Livestock, including cattle, may not be moved or transported unless first inspected by a New Mexico Livestock Board inspector, and the certificate travels with the animals; moves entirely on land the mover controls are exempt (NMSA 1978 § 77-9-41). Anyone moving livestock from one district to another or out of the state must first notify the district inspector (21.32.3.9 NMAC). Auction and slaughter sales are inspected, and a bill of sale is required on a change of ownership (21.32.3 NMAC). The rule in 21.32.10 NMAC lists cattle and bison inspection at $0.50 per head; the Livestock Board's fee page says that rises to $0.60 per head, with the service charge going from $10 to $15, from January 1, 2026. Confirm current fees with the Livestock Board.
