Lien rules by state · North Dakota

North Dakota cattle lien rules.

Central filing. North Dakota is on USDA's list of states with a certified central filing system (certified December 16, 1986; products covered: "All"), run by the Secretary of State, which produces a separate list for livestock.

System

Central filing. USDA lists North Dakota as certified on December 16, 1986, with products covered shown as "All". The Secretary of State keeps a computerized central indexing system and produces a list for crops and a list for livestock, including central notice financing statements and agister's, processor's and supplier's liens (N.D.C.C. 54-09-09, 54-09-10). All filings must be electronic (41-09-72). A central notice (CNS) statement must describe the property, including the county, and be amended within three months of a material change (41-09-73). The Secretary of State says the CNS statement is in addition to the standard UCC financing statement, which must also be filed to perfect against other creditors. Filing costs $40, continuation $30 (41-09-96).

What a buyer must do

A buyer of cattle takes subject to the lender's lien if it failed to register with the state's central filing system before buying and the lender had filed an effective financing statement covering the cattle (7 U.S.C. § 1631(e)(2)), or if it received the state's written notice naming the seller and product and did not secure a waiver or release (§ 1631(e)(3)). Sale barns selling for others: § 1631(g). North Dakota's UCC (N.D.C.C. 41-09-40) leaves buyers of farm products from farmers out of its ordinary buyer rule and sets its own: a lender must be on the most current Secretary of State list to hold a livestock buyer liable; the buyer must make the check payable jointly to the farmer and every secured party or lienholder on the current list when the check is issued; and the buyer takes free if it did that, or if the lien or the seller is not on the current list. A lender suing a buyer must first meet conditions in 41-09-40, including a judgment against the farmer and a notice to the buyer within 18 months of the check date. Buyers get the list through a Secretary of State buyer's report subscription ($40 a month or $480 a year, updated on the 1st of each month), bought in the state's NDCIS system; we found no separate buyer registration fee.

Liens that can outrank a lender

An agister's lien for feeding, herding or pasturing (N.D.C.C. ch. 35-17), filed in the central indexing system within 90 days of taking possession, has priority over other liens except agricultural processors' or suppliers' liens (35-17-03, 35-17-04). The agricultural supplier's lien (ch. 35-31) covers feed, hay, pasturage and veterinary services and must be filed within 120 days; its priority section (35-31-03) speaks of "crops or agricultural products". We found no separate veterinarian's lien chapter.

Searching financing statements

The Secretary of State's public search (no login, not certified) covers UCC, central notice (CNS), agricultural statutory lien (ASL) and tax lien indexes by debtor name or filing number. Electronic debtor searches, including certified UCC-11 searches, cost nothing; a secured-party search costs $500 (41-09-96; Secretary of State lien searches page). A certificate on central notice information costs $15 and a listing $40 per record requested (54-09-11).

Brand inspection

Yes, statewide. The North Dakota Stockmen's Association inspects cattle, horses and mules shipped or consigned to any livestock auction market, buying station or packing plant in the state (N.D.C.C. 4.1-72-01). Cattle may not leave the state without an inspection and certificate of ownership, except for emergency veterinary care or shipment to an approved market in a bordering state, and may not leave a market until inspected (4.1-73-21); a violation is a class A misdemeanor, a class C felony on repeat. A seller of branded livestock must give a bill of sale within 15 days unless an inspector issued a certificate of ownership (4.1-73-18). The fee is $1.50 per head, withheld from sale proceeds, plus mileage when the inspector travels (N.D. Admin. Code 48.1-03-01-03). Recording a brand costs $50 (4.1-73-14); brands expire every five years from January 1, 2016 (4.1-73-10).
