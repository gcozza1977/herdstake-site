Lien rules by state · Oregon

Oregon cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Oregon among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All"). The Secretary of State runs the farm products system under ORS 81.230 to 81.263 (renumbered from ORS 80.100 to 80.130 in 2025).

System

Central filing. A lender registers a security interest in farm products, which the statute defines to include cattle, by filing an effective financing statement (EFS) with the Secretary of State (ORS 81.230, 81.260). The EFS does not create or perfect the security interest; the lender still files a UCC financing statement to perfect it (OAR 160-050-0180). An EFS names the secured party, each person subjecting the product to the interest, a unique identifier for each, the product and the Oregon county where it is produced or located, and is signed or authorized by the debtor (ORS 81.248(1)). It must be amended within three months to reflect material changes and stays effective five years, continued by refiling or a continuation statement in the six months before it expires (ORS 81.248(2)-(3)). The Secretary of State compiles a monthly master list of unlapsed filings, which registrants can also search online by debtor name (ORS 81.251; OAR 160-050-0230).

What a buyer must do

A buyer who buys farm products in the ordinary course from a seller engaged in farming takes free of the seller's lender, even if the lien is perfected and the buyer knows of it, except as the central filing rules provide (ORS 81.242). Under those rules a buyer, commission merchant or selling agent is subject to the lender's interest if (1) it did not register with the Secretary of State before the purchase and an EFS covering the product had been filed, (2) it received the master list or a written notice from the Secretary of State naming the seller and the product and did not get a waiver or release from the lender, or (3) it participates in the Secretary of State's computer access program that names the seller and product and did not get a release (ORS 81.245). Registration is with the Secretary of State, effective for one year, and registrants receive the relevant parts of the master list each month for a yearly subscription fee set by rule (ORS 81.251, 81.263). A person who is not registered can ask for oral confirmation of an EFS within 24 hours, followed by written confirmation (ORS 81.254). The federal rule is in 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

The Secretary of State sets the filing, registration, subscription and confirmation fees by rule; the statute says they are nonrefundable and may not exceed the cost of running the system (ORS 81.239, 81.248(5), 81.251(5), 81.254(5), 81.263). I did not find the current amounts at a source I could read, so none is given here. Confirm them with the Secretary of State.

Brand inspection

Yes. Brand inspection and a certificate are required for cattle transported out of Oregon or to range outside the state, and for cattle going to a livestock auction market, sales facility or slaughterhouse in Oregon before sale or slaughter, with exceptions in the statute (ORS 604.046(1), (5)). Cattle moved from the owner's premises to another place in Oregon must carry a transportation certificate, except cattle trailed to or from range in the usual course of ranch operation (ORS 604.061). The statute sets the inspection fee at $1.75 per head of cattle for a certificate valid eight days and $10 per head for a certificate valid longer, plus assessments under ORS 577.512, and lets the department charge a service fee of up to $35 (ORS 604.046(2), 604.066). The department may also set up inspection at changes of ownership (ORS 604.046(3)). Confirm current fees with the Department of Agriculture.
