Lien rules by state · Utah

Utah cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Utah among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All"). The Division of Corporations and Commercial Code runs it under rules the Utah UCC directs it to issue (Utah Code 70A-9a-320(6); Utah Administrative Code R154-1).

System

Central filing. A lender files a central filing system (CFS) statement, which is Utah's version of the federal effective financing statement, to put buyers and selling agents on notice of its security interest in farm products. The Division of Corporations and Commercial Code says the filing is made online at ucc.utah.gov, that a CFS-3 amends, continues or terminates it, and that a CFS-2 requests copies. The collateral must be listed as specific farm products (it gives cattle and calves as an example) with the Utah county where the product will be produced, and all debtors on the original lien must sign.

What a buyer must do

Utah's UCC leaves a person buying farm products from a person engaged in farming operations out of its ordinary buyer rule (Utah Code 70A-9a-320(1)). It adds that a secured party may not enforce a security interest in farm products against a buyer, commission merchant or selling agent who buys or sells them in the ordinary course from or for a farmer unless the secured party complied with the Division's central filing rules (70A-9a-320(6)). A buyer or agent applies each year to register for the master list of filings, through the online system or on form CFS-4, and registrations expire at 5 p.m. on the last business day of the registration year (R154-1-9). The Division sends the master list at the start of each month with an update on the 15th, by email in Excel, on CD or in print for an added cost; a new filing appears in the next edition or update only if filed before 4 p.m. on the 15th (R154-1-11). The federal rule is in 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

The Division's Central Filing System page states a fee of $12.00 per CFS filing. The rule leaves the amount to the state fee schedule (R154-1-5), and a buyer registration fee is not stated on the pages I could read, so none is given here. Confirm current fees with the Division.

Brand inspection

Yes. Ownership of cattle may not be transferred, by sale or otherwise, without a certificate of brand inspection from a Department of Agriculture and Food brand inspector; the exception is dairy calves under 60 days old leaving the farm of origin (Utah Code 4-24-302). Cattle may not be driven or transported out of Utah until brand inspected, except animals that customarily forage on an open range crossing the state line and rodeo stock with a current yearly inspection (4-24-306). Anyone transporting cattle must carry an official state brand certificate or other proof of ownership (4-24-307), and cattle must be brand inspected before slaughter, with exceptions (4-24-304). The Department sets inspection fees with the approval of the Livestock Brand Board (4-24-308); the amount is not in the statute, so confirm it with the Department.

State offices

Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code, Central Filing System (online filing at ucc.utah.gov) · Utah Department of Agriculture and Food
