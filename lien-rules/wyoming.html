Lien rules by state · Wyoming

Wyoming cattle lien rules.

Central filing. USDA lists Wyoming among the states with a certified central filing system (products covered: "All*"; USDA's page does not define the asterisk). The Secretary of State runs it under Wyoming Statutes 34-21-1101 to 34-21-1107.

System

Central filing. The Secretary of State keeps a statewide central filing system for effective financing statements (EFS) on farm products, which the statute defines to include cattle (W.S. 34-21-1101, 34-21-1102). A lender files an EFS that names the debtor, the secured party, the farm product and each county where it is used or produced. An EFS stays effective for five years and can be continued by a filing within the six months before it expires; it must be amended within three months to reflect material changes (W.S. 34-21-1101(a)(v)(E)-(F), 34-21-1103). The Secretary of State must give a buyer oral confirmation of an EFS within 24 hours of a request, and written confirmation on request (W.S. 34-21-1105).

What a buyer must do

For a farm product produced in Wyoming, a buyer in the ordinary course of business takes subject to the lender's security interest if the buyer did not register with the Secretary of State before the purchase and the lender had filed an EFS covering the product, or if the buyer received the Secretary of State's written notice naming the seller and the product and did not get a waiver or release from the lender (W.S. 34-21-1106(a)). Paying with a check or other instrument made out to the seller and to each lender identified in the central filing system, which the lender endorses, counts as a release up to the amount of the instrument. A registrant, or a buyer who made an inquiry under W.S. 34-21-1105, does not take subject to the lien if the system failed to provide information it was required to carry and that kept the buyer from knowing of the EFS (W.S. 34-21-1106(b)). Registration is by calendar year, and registrants receive the master list from the Secretary of State (W.S. 34-21-1102(a)(iv)). The federal rule is in 7 U.S.C. § 1631.

Fees

The Secretary of State's fee schedule (revised May 2021) lists buyer registration, initial or renewal, at no fee and an official search at no fee. EFS on paper: $20 for up to two pages and $40 for more; an amendment $15 for up to two pages and $30 for more; a termination no fee; a certified search of one debtor $10; copies $0.50 a page. The statute adds that an EFS listing more than ten products, counties or combinations of them is charged an additional fee (W.S. 34-21-1101(a)(v)(J)), and fees are set by Secretary of State rule (W.S. 34-21-1107). Confirm current fees with the Secretary of State before you rely on them.

Brand inspection

Yes. Unless an exception applies, it is unlawful to sell, change ownership of, or remove from any Wyoming county to another state or country any livestock that has not been inspected for brands and ownership by an authorized Wyoming brand inspector, with a certificate of inspection or clearance issued (W.S. 11-20-203(a)). Moving livestock from one Wyoming county to another also requires inspection, except where the Livestock Board has set up brand inspection zones covering several counties or the whole state, which it reviews each year (W.S. 11-20-203(c)). No inspection is required for livestock from another state moving through Wyoming in a carrier's custody (W.S. 11-20-209), for certain direct imports with written authority and health papers (W.S. 11-20-210), or for moves to accustomed range on both sides of a county or state line (W.S. 11-20-211). Inspection fees are set by the Livestock Board but not less than $1.25 per head of cattle, plus a $6.25 surcharge per inspection that the board's rules can waive in set conditions (W.S. 11-20-401). Confirm current fees with the Livestock Board.
