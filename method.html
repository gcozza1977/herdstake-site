The check, step by step

Every check follows the same seven steps, and the report records each one.

  1. You send a list: borrower, yard and claimed head.
  2. We match each yard to its state permit for the permitted head capacity and the operator of record.
  3. We get a 30 cm satellite image of the yard, dated as close to the check date as we can, from licensed commercial suppliers.
  4. We count the cattle in each open pen with models built for packed scenes, and give a range at a 90% confidence level.
  5. We cross-check: the claim against the range and the permit, UCC filings that name the borrower's livestock, the state's Clear Title list where one exists, the yard's Packers & Stockyards registration, and court records.
  6. Our collateral review lead reviews the file and signs the report.
  7. You get the report and a spreadsheet within 10 business days of your list.

Nineteen states keep a USDA-certified central list of liens on farm products, Nebraska among them; Texas and Kansas rely instead on lenders giving buyers written notice (USDA AMS). Where there is no central list, the lien check rests on UCC filings.

What the range means

A satellite image shows the cattle standing in open pens at one moment. We count them and report a range, such as 4,150–4,400 head, at a 90% confidence level: on the image date, we expect the true count to fall inside the range about nine times in ten.

A range is more honest than a single number, and it is what every flag rests on. The report names the image date, the pens counted, and any pens we could not count, such as covered barns.

When we flag a gap

We flag a claim when it sits more than 15% above the top of our range. With a range of 4,150–4,400, the flag line is 5,060: a claim of 4,800 is noted on the report but not flagged, and a claim of 6,000 is flagged. Fifteen percent is our starting margin; the blind test will tune it.

A flagged report recommends a physical count by an independent counter, a brand inspector where the state has one or a local vet, within 10 days. We arrange it, and the lender orders and pays the counter's fee.

Reports never use the words fraud or missing cattle. A gap can have ordinary causes: cattle shipped since the borrower's last report, cattle held at another yard, or a pen sheet not yet updated. The physical count settles it.

Accuracy and results

We are testing our counts against real yards before we sell checks at scale. Five open-pen feedyards across Texas, Kansas and Nebraska, at least one under 5,000 head and one over 30,000, share their pen sheets with us. We count from the images first and send the yard our numbers; only then does the yard send its pen sheets for those dates, so our counts are fixed before we see theirs. We start with three to four past dates per yard and may add later dates over the following three months.

Review and sign-off

Software never signs a report. Our collateral review lead reviews every file before release and signs the report. The role requires at least five years in agricultural lending, ag bank examination or livestock collateral inspection, and working knowledge of how Farm Credit and bank examiners judge collateral evaluations.

The reviewer also orders the physical count when a check flags a gap and handles any borrower dispute, through the ordering lender. No one at Herdstake has a stake in any borrower or yard we check.

We haven't found a rule that accepts a satellite count in place of a physical count, or one that rules it out. That is why Herdstake is a check between site visits, with a physical count whenever something looks wrong.

Your data

  • What we need: borrower name, yard and claimed head. No Social Security numbers, financial statements or credit files.
  • How to send it: by email to team@herdstake.com, or through your bank's secure email; we reply the same way.
  • No system access: we never connect to your loan or core systems.
  • Who sees it: only you. Your list and your reports are never shown to another lender, a yard or anyone else.
  • How long we keep it: while you're a customer, so each check can be compared with the last. Deleted within 30 days of your request, or of a pilot that ends without a contract.
  • Imagery: bought under licenses that let you, your auditors and your examiners see the images in your file.

Limits

  • Covered barns and heavy shade. Cattle under a roof can't be counted from above. The report says so when a yard has them.
  • Packed pens. Counting is hardest where animals touch. That is what the range is for.
  • One moment in time. A count covers the image date. Cattle move, ship and arrive between checks.
  • Feedyards first. The pilot covers open feedyard pens. Pasture checks come later.
  • Not an appraisal. We report head and location; you apply your own price.

Published studies of satellite cattle detection have found roughly 77% to 92% of the animals seen on the ground, some with false detections (Cambridge repository, arXiv). That is why we report ranges, cross-check records and send every flag to a physical count.
